# Multisensory Narrative System for Deafblindness and Multiple Disabilities

**Role:** Author of this adaptation — deafblindness and multiple disabilities
**Shared base concept:** developed with Iris Góngora, who authored the early-childhood adaptation
**Program:** Maestría en Educación Especial, mención Discapacidad Múltiple — Universidad Politécnica
Salesiana, Ecuador
**Status:** Design phase

---

## The challenge

Stories are how most children first learn that the world has causes, sequences, and other people in it.
A student with deafblindness and multiple disabilities is routinely left outside that, not because the
story is too complex but because every channel it arrives through — print, picture, spoken voice — is a
channel they cannot fully use.

The usual response is to simplify the story. That solves the wrong problem. The story was never the
barrier; the delivery was.

## The approach

We designed a narrative system that delivers stories through all eight sensory systems rather than the
two that print media assumes: visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory, gustatory, and — the three usually
left out of "multisensory" resources — vestibular, proprioceptive, and interoceptive.

Generative AI produces and adapts the stories themselves, tuned to the learner's profile, which is what
makes the system reproducible rather than a one-off craft project. The explicit design constraint is that
AI does not replace pedagogical mediation: it generates material, and the teacher, mediator, or family
member carries the interaction.

The prototype deliberately pairs digital resources with physical objects. A story that exists only on a
screen is inaccessible to a learner who reads the world through their hands.

## What I delivered

- Pedagogical framework grounding the system in constructivist, sociocultural, and UDL principles
- Design of the eight-sense integration model for narrative activities
- Specification for AI-generated adapted stories with defined learner-profile inputs
- Prototype architecture combining digital and physical materials
- Participation model for teachers, families, and support professionals

## What this demonstrates

Designing for the learners most often designed around, and treating accessibility as a question about
channels rather than about difficulty. It also shows generative AI positioned where it belongs — producing
adaptable material at scale while a human carries the relationship that makes the material mean anything.

**Collaboration note:** the base concept — narrative as a multisensory access route, with generative AI
producing the adapted stories — was developed jointly with Iris Góngora. We then took it into our
respective fields: she authored the early-childhood version, and this adaptation for learners with
deafblindness and multiple disabilities, including the eight-sense integration model and the pedagogical
framework, is my own.
