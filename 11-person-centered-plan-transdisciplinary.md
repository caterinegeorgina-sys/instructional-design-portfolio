# Person-Centered Plan: A Transdisciplinary Model

**Role:** Special education lead and process coordinator
**Team:** Six-person transdisciplinary team — physical therapy, speech-language pathology, physical
education, early childhood education, language pedagogy — plus the family and an equine therapy team
**Program:** Maestría en Educación Especial, mención Discapacidad Múltiple — Universidad Politécnica
Salesiana, Ecuador
**Instruments:** Person-Centered Planning · Rowland Communication Matrix · ICF functional framework

---

## The challenge

A child with multiple disabilities is usually surrounded by professionals who each see one slice of her.
The physical therapist knows her posture, the speech pathologist knows her communication signals, the
teacher knows her classroom day, the family knows everything the others never see — and none of it adds
up to a single plan.

What the child gets instead is six good interventions that quietly contradict each other, and a file that
describes her by what she cannot do.

## The approach

I coordinated a person-centered plan built to resolve exactly that. Person-centered planning puts the
person and her environment at the center of educational decisions rather than the diagnosis, and the
transdisciplinary model requires the professionals to share objectives rather than merely report to one
another.

The communication work is grounded in the Rowland Communication Matrix, which is the right instrument
here because it describes pre-symbolic communication — behavior, gesture, body signals — as communication
rather than as its absence. That reframing is the whole argument: a child who has no words is not a child
without intent.

The ICF functional framework supplies the structure, so that the plan describes participation and
environmental barriers rather than cataloguing deficits. Equine therapy is integrated as a complementary
intervention for postural, emotional, and sensory regulation, with its observations fed back to the rest
of the team so the gains generalize into the classroom and the home.

The family is treated as a member of the team with decision-making standing, not as recipients of a plan
written about their child.

## What I delivered

- Coordination of the full evaluation and planning process across six professional roles
- Functional assessment of expressive communication via the Rowland Communication Matrix
- Person-centered profile organized around strengths, participation, and quality of life
- Goal plan with implementation activities, assigned responsibilities, and follow-up criteria
- Structure for generalizing strategies between school and home

## What this demonstrates

Leading a plan across disciplines that do not naturally share vocabulary, and turning that into one
coherent document people can actually act on. It also shows assessment used the way it should be — to
find the access route rather than to measure the distance from a norm.

**On confidentiality:** the student, her family, and the professional team are not identified in the
published document. Names, dates of birth, record numbers, and the acknowledgments section have been
removed. What remains is the method.
