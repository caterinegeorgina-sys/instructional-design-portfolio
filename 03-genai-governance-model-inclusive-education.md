# Generative AI Governance Model for Educational Decision-Making

**Role:** Strategy and instructional systems designer
**Program:** Diplomado en Inteligencia Artificial Generativa — Asturias Corporación Universitaria
**Frameworks:** Universal Design for Learning · organizational risk management

---

## The challenge

Generative AI arrived in education faster than any framework for governing it. Institutions face a real
dilemma: the tools can meaningfully reduce workload in planning, materials creation, and decision support,
but deployed without structure they introduce risks around accuracy, equity, data handling, and the
erosion of professional judgment.

The gap is not enthusiasm. It is the absence of a model that says who decides what, where human validation
is mandatory, and how the institution answers for the output.

## The approach

I built a strategic model for integrating generative AI across pedagogical, analytical, and organizational
processes, aimed specifically at inclusive education settings and services for students with multiple
disabilities.

The model pairs language-model tooling with UDL principles rather than treating them separately. This
matters: AI can generate multiple representations of the same content at near-zero marginal cost, which is
exactly what UDL asks for and what has always been too expensive to do at scale. The design exploits that
alignment deliberately.

Human validation is not a recommendation in this model. It is a required step in every workflow, with
defined decision points where professional judgment cannot be delegated.

## What I delivered

- Executive summary and strategic rationale grounded in current literature
- Implementation model covering pedagogical planning, materials generation, and organizational
  information management
- Governance framework defining roles, decision rights, and mandatory human validation checkpoints
- Assessment of ethical, legal, and organizational risk
- Viability analysis for institutional adoption

## What this demonstrates

Systems-level thinking beyond single-course design. This is the work of someone who can sit with a
learning and development director and discuss adoption strategy, risk posture, and governance, not only
build the modules once those decisions have been made.
