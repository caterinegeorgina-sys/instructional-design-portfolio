# Accessibility Audit of Classroom Learning Materials

**Role:** Accessibility analyst · **Context:** Special education classroom, junior high level, Utah
**Frameworks:** WCAG 2.1 · Universal Design for Learning

---

> **Note on the case profile:** "Ana" is an illustrative composite profile used to ground the design decisions in a concrete set of needs. She is not an identifiable student, and no school is named.

---

## The challenge

Materials that "work fine" are rarely audited. A pictogram communication card system had been in daily
use across every routine transition, choice moment, and communication activity in a classroom serving
students with cerebral palsy, autism spectrum disorders, and intellectual disability. It existed as
printed cards plus a static PDF sent home to families.

Nobody had ever checked whether it was accessible, precisely because it looked simple. That is the most
common form of accessibility failure in education: not a resource that is obviously broken, but one that
functions correctly on screen for the adult using it while quietly excluding the learner it was built for.

## The approach

I evaluated the material against the four WCAG principles and the guidelines most relevant to a special
education context, scoring each criterion as compliant, partial, or absent, and pairing every finding with
a concrete remediation.

The audit deliberately covered both formats, printed and digital, because accessibility gaps behave
differently in each. A colour contrast problem persists on paper; a missing semantic structure only
matters in the PDF.

## What I found

Five of eight evaluated criteria failed. The most consequential:

- **No alternative text** on any pictogram, making the digital version unreadable by screen readers
- **Insufficient colour contrast** on several cards, with low-saturation pictograms on light backgrounds
  falling below the 4.5:1 minimum
- **No multimodal alternative** — the system was visual only, with no auditory channel
- **No keyboard or assistive technology access** in the static PDF
- **No semantic structure** in the document distributed to families

## What I delivered

A structured audit report with a criterion-by-criterion evaluation table, a barrier inventory mapped to
the specific student populations affected, and a phased improvement plan covering accessible redesign,
multimodal versioning, and alternative access routes. Each proposed fix named the tool or technique to
apply, from contrast checkers such as WAVE and Colour Contrast Analyser to specific alternative text
patterns.

The plan was not left on paper. Phases one through three became the working resource in project 01.

## What this demonstrates

Evaluation discipline: the ability to take an existing asset, measure it against a standard rather than an
opinion, and produce findings specific enough to act on. This is the analysis phase of ADDIE applied to
accessibility, and it is the step most often skipped.
