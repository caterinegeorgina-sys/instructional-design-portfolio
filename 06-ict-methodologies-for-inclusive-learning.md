# ICT-Based Methodologies for Inclusive Learning

**Role:** Instructional designer · **Course:** Innovación Digital en la Práctica Docente
**Focus:** Active learning methodologies for students with multiple disabilities

---

## The challenge

Active methodologies — exploration, inquiry, collaboration, problem-solving — are the consensus answer to
what good teaching looks like. They are also written, almost without exception, for students who can
already move, speak, and self-direct.

Students with multiple disabilities are the population most often excluded from student-centered
approaches, and the exclusion is rarely deliberate. It is a design oversight repeated so consistently that
it stops looking like one.

## The approach

I analyzed methodologies currently considered innovative in education, assessed their pedagogical
potential for meaningful learning, and worked through what each one actually requires of a learner:
which capacities it assumes, and which of those assumptions break for a student with complex support
needs.

From there I identified how ICT tools can carry the load an assumption makes impossible — where a
switch, a communication device, or an adapted interface substitutes for the movement, speech, or
independent initiation the methodology took for granted.

## What I delivered

- Analysis of current innovative methodologies and their pedagogical basis
- Assessment of each methodology's potential to promote meaningful learning
- Technology selection criteria mapped to specific learner support needs
- Application design for inclusive and special education contexts

## What this demonstrates

The habit of reading a framework critically instead of adopting it wholesale. Most instructional designers
can apply a methodology. Fewer can identify which learners it silently excludes and redesign the entry
point so the methodology still holds.
